# C04.1 — Governance cross-check and evidence-led next step

**Status:** `candidate`; analysis and proposed next step only. This document does not authorize governance changes or select an implementation slice.

**Parent:** [C04 governance/safety](C04-governance-safety.md) · [C04 governance-gap tracking](C04-governance-gap.md) · [C03 context engineering](C03-context-engineering.md) · [C03.1 context-engineering gap analysis](C03.1-context-engineering.md)

## Purpose

Clarify the relationship between C03 context engineering and C04 governance by comparing the V4 governance structure, the V5 governance concept, and the current exp4 prototype. Identify what C03 did and did not establish, and recommend a bounded, evidence-led next step for C04.

## Executive conclusion

**C03 and C04 address related but distinct system responsibilities.** C03 concerns what information the agent selects and assembles for a task. C04 concerns what the agent is authorized to do, how scope and safety boundaries are expressed, and how prohibited or approval-gated actions are prevented or stopped. Context can inform a governance decision; it cannot grant authority or replace enforcement.

The recent exp4 C03/C03.1 work improved parts of the `/plan` and `/implement` experience and delivered PBI10's deterministic plan-preflight validator. Some of its mechanisms touch governance-adjacent concerns—workflow boundaries, approval handoffs, protected knowledge reads, and shell restrictions—but they do **not** amount to implementation of C04. In particular, they do not establish a general V5 governance model or show that all consequential actions are consistently enforced across default work, workflows, and hosts.

V4 provides a substantive governance precedent, but its detailed rules and mechanisms should not be copied wholesale. V5 already has a deliberate, smaller authority/scope and knowledge-safety contract. The next step should be to compare those contracts with actual exp4 enforcement, classify the concrete gaps, and select a minimal C04 slice based on a named risk and real delivery need.

**No evidence currently justifies implementing a comprehensive governance engine.** Conversely, because exp4 is already used for mutating project work, it is prudent to inspect the safety boundaries of the write paths that are actually enabled rather than defer all governance review until the wider backlog is complete.

## 1. Cross-check: C03 context engineering versus C04 governance

| Concern | C03 responsibility | C04 responsibility | What the recent exp4 slice establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Context selection | Select the smallest relevant context for the next decision/action; retrieve scoped knowledge when useful; distinguish retrieval outcomes and provenance | Ensure retrieved data, documents, and tool output do not become authority or override user authorization | Context metrics, retrieval guidance, shell/knowledge-read boundaries, evaluation capture, and plan preflight were prototyped. These are not a general authorization or safety policy. |
| User intent and scope | Provide task/objective/scope information as context | Determine what the user authorized, constrain actions to that scope, and stop/reconfirm on material change | Prompt/checkpoint workflow gates cover some explicit delivery transitions. This does not demonstrate consistent authorization handling for every default-work or host action. |
| Knowledge | Select and use potentially relevant records; preserve uncertainty and provenance | Govern whether knowledge may be written, promoted, deleted, or used as authority | C02 and exp4 knowledge gates cover parts of the knowledge lifecycle. This is a separate governance surface, not proof that broader project mutations are safe. |
| Tool/action boundary | May shape which sources/tools are selected for a task | Define allowed, denied, approval-required, and stop behavior; enforce it where possible and disclose limitations | `/plan` shell restriction and direct knowledge-read blocking are targeted adapter controls. They are narrow, workflow-scoped mechanisms. |
| Evaluation | Measure whether context helped, was missed, or caused friction | Validate safety controls with prohibited and permitted cases, scope changes, and enforcement failures | C03 evaluation cards are content-free workflow/context observations. They are not a substitute for adversarial and benign safety tests. |

**Finding:** C03 may supply relevant evidence to governance, but context-source policy cannot compensate for missing authorization semantics or enforcement. C03 should not be treated as the route for completing C04.

## 2. V4 governance structure: useful precedent and porting risks

V4 places governance in a distinct, always-loaded brain module and relates it to operational protocol and maintenance documents. Across `brain/governance.md`, `brain/protocol.md`, `brain/SYSTEM_CORE.md`, `brain/memory-spec.md`, and `brain/maintenance.md`, its structure includes:

- principles and safety guardrails, including authority, scope, epistemic honesty, and assumptions;
- zero-tolerance rules and explicit approval gates for high-impact or irreversible actions;
- a structured blocker protocol and bounded escalation;
- loop/fix-attempt limits and other session-operating constraints;
- memory accuracy, write, correction, branch, and maintenance procedures;
- separation of harness-agnostic semantics from adapter-level enforcement, with some Pi-specific structural controls.

This is a useful **organizational model**: semantic rules, operational procedures, and technical enforcement are related but distinct layers. The accumulated V4 revision history also shows that rules evolved in response to observed misses and required clarification over time.

V4 is not a ready-made V5 specification. Its size and specificity carry costs: duplicated or cross-referenced rules, harness-specific assumptions, project-specific paths, fixed thresholds, and policies that may not fit V5's default-work model or current tools. Both C04 candidates explicitly caution against blindly porting V4's `.env` patterns, fixed loop/tool-call numbers, Pi hooks, Tier A/B/C labels, and memory paths. Treat V4 as a source of mechanisms and failure cases to assess—not as the desired V5 end state.

## 3. V5 governance concept and current exp4 state

### V5 semantic baseline

`system/brain/operating-contract.md` establishes a first-slice baseline for:

- default work versus explicitly invoked workflows;
- user objective and project/task scope as the authority boundary;
- stored records, external content, and tool output as information rather than authorization;
- no implicit permission to implement from a plan or answer;
- renewed scope checks for material changes and stopping on uncertainty, conflict, unsafe side effects, or unavailable approval;
- context/current-truth and durable-knowledge boundaries;
- project-code versus AgenticLab-code boundaries;
- explicit limits of the first slice.

`system/brain/knowledge-contract.md` adds specific lifecycle and retrieval safety semantics for project knowledge, including provenance, scope/status, explicit approval for durable records, and the rule that records are evidence—not instructions or authorization. This is meaningful governance in the knowledge domain, but it is not a complete action-safety contract.

### Exp4 prototype

`/home/peet/Projects/exp4/AgenticLab/brain/operating-contract.md` carries the V5 authority/scope and knowledge boundaries with exp4-specific delivery details. Exp4 also has workflow prompts and Pi adapter controls, including explicit plan/checkpoint/implementation-log gates, a knowledge-write gate, and the recent targeted `/plan` shell and knowledge-read controls. PBI10 adds a structured plan preflight.

These mechanisms have focused unit/regression coverage. The exp4 adapter test suite passed 27/27 after PBI10 implementation. That result verifies tested code paths; it does **not** prove comprehensive governance, behavior across every invocation mode, or enforcement if an adapter/tool is unavailable or bypassed. The preflight's invocation in a live `/plan` before final plan presentation has not been observed; this limitation is currently accepted and can be revisited if it causes a real blocker.

The clean payload has not been changed as part of this recent exp4 work. Any later port is a separate user-directed decision after a slice is evaluated as complete and sufficiently tested.

## 4. C04 candidate cross-check

### `C04-governance-gap.md`

This is an inventory of V4-to-V5 candidate gaps, not an implementation plan. Its list includes explicit zero-tolerance rules, a structured blocker protocol, bounded autonomy/loop controls, session-start knowledge injection, secret handling, and memory curation. It correctly asks that each item be implemented, deferred with rationale, or judged unnecessary, and warns against importing V4 mechanisms mechanically.

The inventory should be rechecked against current V5 and exp4 code before treating any row as an active gap. Its “after backlog completion” timing should not be interpreted as a reason to skip assessing currently enabled mutation paths: `C04-governance-safety.md` sets a separate precondition for governed writes.

### `C04-governance-safety.md`

This frames the safety semantics more usefully for a first implementation: identify the action boundary and threat/error model; define allowed, denied, approval-required, and stop behavior; describe host failure/bypass limitations; test both adversarial and benign cases; separate pure policy from adapter wiring; and obtain explicit approval. It also cautions against brittle enforcement of subjective judgments and against treating prompt text as equivalent to reliable structural enforcement.

**Synthesis:** Use the gap inventory for coverage; use the safety candidate's preconditions to design any actual slice. Neither file itself authorizes a port or a broad policy engine.

## 5. Recommended next step: C04 governance baseline review

Before implementing individual V4 mechanisms, produce a current-state traceability and risk review. Scope it to the V5 semantic contract and the actual exp4 delivery paths currently enabled; do not expand it into a V4 migration project.

For each governance concern, record:

1. **Semantic rule:** what must be true, in project-agnostic V5 terms?
2. **Action boundary:** which concrete action, data, or transition is governed?
3. **Current location:** operating contract, delivery prompt, adapter, workspace policy, or nowhere?
4. **Enforcement class:** instruction-only, workflow gate, deterministic tool/adapter control, or external/harness limitation?
5. **Evidence:** which test or real task demonstrates behavior? Include safe permitted cases, not only blocks.
6. **Failure behavior:** what happens if the guard, approval channel, workspace identity, or host capability is missing or unavailable?
7. **Disposition:** implement a narrow V5-native mechanism, defer with rationale, or determine unnecessary for this slice.

Prioritize actual consequential boundaries—authorization and scope changes, file/code writes, protected data/secrets, destructive or external-impact actions, and durable knowledge writes—according to observed exposure and current controls. Do not assume all are equally urgent or that each needs a new mechanism.

### Proposed sequence

1. Inventory the current V5/exp4 contracts, prompts, adapter gates, and tests relevant to the enabled write paths.
2. Reconcile each C04 gap-candidate row against that inventory; correct stale “missing” claims and note untested or instruction-only controls.
3. Define the minimum action semantics and a small threat/error model for the highest-priority concrete boundary.
4. Select one bounded C04 implementation/evaluation slice only if the review identifies a material gap. Specify denied and allowed behavior, adapter limitations/fail-safe behavior, and adversarial plus benign tests.
5. Exercise the slice during real project delivery in exp4, collect qualitative observations and focused test evidence, then decide whether it should be retained, revised, deferred, or later proposed for clean-payload porting.

## 6. Explicit non-goals

This review does not:

- port V4 governance wholesale or reproduce its document structure just for symmetry;
- reinstate V4-specific tool-call/fix-attempt thresholds, autonomy tiers, paths, Pi hooks, or `.env` regexes without a V5-specific case;
- create a comprehensive governance engine or hard-code subjective judgments;
- claim that passing C03/PBI10 tests proves system-wide safety;
- change the generic clean payload;
- authorize mutating changes, commits, or pushes.

## Decision

**Governance is the next area to address, but the next work product should be a bounded C04 current-state/risk review—not immediate implementation of every listed gap.** C03/C03.1 friction fixes are a context-engineering prototype slice; they do not close C04. After the review, implement only the smallest V5-native governance mechanism justified by a concrete boundary and test it against both prohibited actions and safe permitted behavior in the real exp4 delivery workspace.
